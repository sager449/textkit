What are Arabic diacritics?

Arabic diacritics — known as tashkeel (تشكيل) or harakat (حركات) — are small marks placed above or below Arabic letters to indicate short vowels and pronunciation. They include:

  • Fatha (َ) — short "a" sound
  • Damma (ُ) — short "u" sound
  • Kasra (ِ) — short "i" sound
  • Sukoon (ْ) — no vowel
  • Shadda (ّ) — doubled consonant
  • Tanween (ً ٌ ٍ) — adds "an", "un", "in"

While tashkeel improves readability and is essential in the Quran and poetry, it becomes a problem in modern contexts: search engines, databases, NLP models, and social media often treat diacritics as noise.
